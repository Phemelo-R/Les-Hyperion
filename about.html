B.Sc (Hons) Biodiversity and Conservation

I am an Honours graduate in Biodiversity and Conservation at the University of the Western Cape with a focus on remote sensing and spatial ecology. My Honours research focused on monitoring leucadendron argenteum (Silvertree) populations on Table Mountain using NASA BioSCape's AVIRIS-NG hyperspectral imagery and LVIS LiDAR. I developed a probability map of Silver Tree presence across the reserve using machine learning models trained on field-validated presence records.

My interests lie in using geospatial data and ecological modelling to understand and conserve biodiversity. I spend most of my time working with satellite imagery, building reproducible workflows that move from raw data to ecological inference. Most of my work is documented as Jupyter notebooks that others can follow and adapt using R and Python. I also have experience with GIS software like QGIS for spatial analysis and visualization.

Cape Flats Nature Reserve
My volunteer work at the Cape Flats Nature Reserve involved a range of activities focused on habitat restoration and biodiversity monitoring. I participated in nursery maintenance, which included tasks such as weeding, watering, and preparing seedlings for planting. Additionally, I was involved in clearing invasive alien plants to help restore native vegetation and expanding firebreaks for a prepared burn. One of the most exciting aspects of my volunteer work was conducting biodiversity surveys where we set up mammal traps and invertebrate pitfall traps to monitor local fauna. One of the highlights was a night survey that revealed a variety of nocturnal creatures, including scorpions, a chameleon, and a large spider.
